# Title screen button colors: party findings, 2026-11-25

Owner's ask (Naftali, 2026-11-25 01:12Z to 01:18Z): "Why did continue and new
tower buttons on splash change to something different now?", "All the buttons
seem to be the same color now", "What about with all the other appearance
designs? I feel like they shouldn't all be grey, no?", then "Ask proper party
of gamers, design, and computer historian agents" and "And then come back to
me". The party recommends; the owner picks. Nothing is built before he does.

## What the room was shown

- `computer-before-after.png`: before the desk (up to 4.5.x) Continue was an
  amber plate and the others cream plates on the dusk sky. Since the desk
  (web#2336, 2026-11-15) the title screen sits in a window and every button is
  a gray 1994 push button; the main one is bold with a black ring.
- `phone-before-after.png`: live 4.16.0 phones still show amber and cream;
  main (option B, web#2617, unreleased, in 4.17.0) puts the phone in the window
  too, so its buttons go gray.
- `schemes-today.png`: in the window, Skyline's buttons are its soft light
  gradient and High Contrast's are black with a white edge. No scheme marks the
  main button by color. Away from the window (small browser windows) the
  schemes already do: Skyline's main button is violet, High Contrast's yellow.
- Nobody proposed the gray on purpose: web#2336 said only that the title
  screen "sits in its own Verticopolis window", and no mockup or note shows a
  button-color choice.

## Who was in the room

Web Game Room (Sally, Juno, Samus, Wren, Priya, Lucia, Grumbal, Bea), Kai and
Kenji from the Player Panel, and Midge, a computer historian who turned up for
this round.

## The round

🗄️ **Midge:** For the record, a 1995 push button had no color of its own. Its
face was whatever the user's scheme said "3D objects" were. The default
button was told apart by a heavier black frame, nothing else. So today's
Classic screen is period correct, to the pixel.

🏢 **Kenji:** And the 1994 game opened on a title picture and then a plain
gray system dialog. Nobody missed the color.

🌐 **Priya:** I miss the color. I open a tab at lunch, I see four identical
gray bricks, and I read all four to find Continue. The yellow one said "you,
press me" before I'd read a word.

🤖 **Lucia:** On a phone it's worse. The black ring is one pixel. On a bus
with the brightness down, all four look the same. The yellow was the thumb's
target.

🎨 **Sally:** That's the real problem. The main action lost its signal, and
it lost it by accident, not by a decision. A title screen has one job: get
you back into your tower.

🗄️ **Midge:** Fair, but a launcher isn't a dialog. The CD-ROM autorun screens
of 1995 were all painted art with colored buttons. Games never used system
buttons on their front door.

🎲 **Samus:** So the gray bar is the system part and the poster is ours. Do we
move the buttons back onto the poster, autorun style?

📐 **Juno:** No. Option B put them in the gray bar on purpose, so the poster
stays whole on a phone and the buttons sit where a thumb reaches. Moving them
again reopens a layout Naftali just picked. Keep the bar, color one button.

😤 **Grumbal:** One yellow button in a gray dialog looks like a bug. Somebody
will file "Continue is highlighted, is it stuck?"

♿ **Wren:** Not if the ring and the bold stay. Color must never be the only
cue, and here it wouldn't be: bold text, the black frame, and the color. Amber
#ffc94a with the plate ink #20204a is well past 7 to 1. High Contrast
already uses yellow for "this is the one" everywhere else; its title screen is
the odd one out today.

🖥️ **Kai:** And the schemes already wrote the answer. Away from the window,
Skyline's main button is violet and High Contrast's is yellow. Inside the
window those rules just don't reach. That's an omission, not a design.

📐 **Juno:** Right. One rule: the main title button wears the scheme's accent.
Classic amber (the sun, POLIS, the old plate), Skyline its violet title color,
High Contrast yellow on black. The other buttons keep the scheme's face.

🗄️ **Midge:** My condition: only the title screen. Every other dialog in the
game keeps the plain 1995 default button. The front door may be painted; the
rooms inside stay period.

🏢 **Kenji:** I can live with that. The original's box art was loud too.

😤 **Grumbal:** Then pressed and focused states have to be drawn, or the
yellow one will look broken the first time someone taps it.

📣 **Bea:** And the release note says it plainly: "The main button on the
opening screen is colored again." Players who saw it go gray get it back
without a puzzle.

🌐 **Priya:** And bring cream back for the others? (Option C)

🎨 **Sally:** No. Cream plates on gray chrome fight the window. One colored
button is the signal; four colored buttons are noise again.

## Options put to the vote

- **A. Keep it as is:** every button the scheme's plain face; the main one
  marked by bold and the black ring only.
- **B. The main button wears the scheme's accent:** Classic amber, Skyline
  violet, High Contrast yellow; the other buttons stay the scheme's face; bold
  and the black ring stay; computers and phones alike; only the title screen.
- **C. Classic goes back to the old plates:** amber main, cream for the
  others, inside the gray window; Skyline and High Contrast as B.
- **D. Autorun style:** buttons move back onto the poster as painted plates.

## Verdict (vote 8 to 3 to 0 to 0)

**B** (Sally, Juno, Samus, Wren, Priya, Lucia, Kai, Bea). **A** (Midge,
Kenji, Grumbal). Nobody voted C or D.

Conditions carried with B:

1. The black default ring and the bold text stay, so color is never the only
   cue (Wren).
2. Pressed and keyboard-focus states drawn for the colored button in every
   scheme (Grumbal).
3. Only the title screen's main button. Every other dialog keeps the plain
   1994 default button (Midge).
4. Same rule on computers and phones; the small-window title screen already
   follows it and stays as it is (Kai, Juno).
5. A player-facing note that says it in one line (Bea).

Dissent: Midge and Kenji hold that gray is the faithful 1994 look; Grumbal
holds that a lone colored button reads as a stuck state. Owner decides.
